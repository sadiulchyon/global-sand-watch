    Method and limits

    How aggregation works

    Each member watch runs its own collection, its own editorial policy and its own public archive. Once a day, after the member collectors finish, Global Sand Watch reads each live watch's published archive.json, maps it to a shared record shape with a small adapter, and rebuilds this site. Summaries, dates, figures and review decisions are copied unchanged. Nothing here is re-written, re-translated or re-verified. If one watch is unreachable, its previous records are kept and the watch is marked stale.

    Review status is not the same everywhere

    Bangladesh Sand Watch publishes only records it has accepted. Mekong Sand Watch publishes pilot and imported records whose summaries and translations still await owner and native-language review. The review field on every record shows which applies. Acceptance by a member watch is approval for inclusion and faithful source representation; it does not independently verify allegations.

    Reading the counts

    • Counts are of sources, not of mining incidents. Several reports can describe one event; one study can cover many sites.
    • Coverage is uneven. Bangladesh is the only GBM country covered so far, and the Mekong collection leans toward Viet Nam. A country with no records may simply have no watch yet.
    • Themes are a coarse, rule-based index added here. Member topics are kept as written.
    • Bangladesh records in the Karnaphuli, Halda, Sangu, Matamuhuri, Bakkhali, Naf and Maheshkhali systems are placed in a separate south-eastern basin by their named river. This assignment is a heuristic.

    Joining the network

    A new watch publishes a static archive.json and is added to watches.json with an adapter. New watches, starting with Kenya and Rwanda, can publish directly in the shared record shape documented in the repository, so no adapter is needed.
